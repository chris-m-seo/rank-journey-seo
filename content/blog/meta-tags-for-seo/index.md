## Introduction

You launched a new page to your site. The page ranks on search engines. You see the impression number rising on Google Search Console, the free tool that shows you how your pages perform in search. But no one is clicking through to your page.

Sound familiar?

This is a common experience for people new to e-com and publishing online.

If your website is ranking on search engines, getting impressions but zero clicks, that can mean your meta tags aren’t yet optimised for your audience or search engines.

Your meta title and meta description (your meta tags) are the headline and short description that appear for your page in search results.

Optimising your landing page meta tags for SEO begins in your HTML head section. The HTML head section is where search engines begin to read your page and discover what the subject and intent is about. 

Think of it as the flag you wave for your website.

It's the first impression your website makes on search engines. In the same way you're reading this page from the top down, search engine crawlers read your page in the same way. Crawlers read the HTML code used to make your page as well as the on-page text. The head section comes first in your page code, which is what makes the head section important. It's one of the first things crawlers read.

## Meta Tags in HTML for SEO

The meta tags in your HTML head section are often used by search engines on the *Search Engine Results Page* (SERP). The meta tags in the head section that are most important for SEO, and that you will likely encounter most often, are the meta title and meta description.

They usually look like this in HTML, depending on your Content Management System (CMS):

```html
<head>
<title>Page Title Here</title>
<meta name="description" content="Meta description goes here">
</head>
```

These two meta tags help to define your content to search engines. They give an early clue as to what is on your page, and how your new page relates to other pages on your site. If your landing pages are struggling to drive traffic to your site, your meta tags are a good place to start investigating as to why that might be the case.

## **Meta titles**

The most important of the meta tags is the meta title. Most SEO experts agree that the title tag is one of the most important elements on a webpage. The meta title tag not only shows up in your browser tab, but also the SERP. 

Because of this, it is thought that the title tag influences the search engine rankings and algorithms. The benefits of optimising your title tags can be increased rankings, better branding on new sites, and clicks to your page. Getting it wrong can have the opposite effect. If your meta title doesn't accurately reflect your page content, it's not uncommon for search engines to rewrite it entirely, so the title you carefully wrote isn't used at all.

Title tags that appear on more than one of your website pages are also a problem for search engines. Write a clear and descriptive title for each page. If several of your pages have the same title, search engines may rewrite it so that people using the search engine can tell each page apart. 

What this means for you is that you lose control of how your page appears in search results. Copying someone else's title creates a similar issue. A non-descriptive title copied from another website weakens your position and doesn't give searchers a reason to choose you over your competition.

Poorly written title tags won't attract click-throughs to your page, or help your site's branding, so it is worth spending time making your title tag sound right.

Your meta title isn’t the same as your page’s H1, the headline people see on the page. They work as a pair. I cover the H1 side in [how to write and optimise H1 tags for SEO](https://www.rankjourneyseo.com/articles/optimise-h1-tags-for-seo/).

### **How long should a meta title be?**

A truncated title on your page's search result is...

See how annoying being cut off is?

That is what a truncated title looks and feels like on a search engine results page. 

It's easy to see from that example why people don't click on those pages. Getting the length of your meta titles right can have a big impact on driving visitors to your site.

Although short, the meta title tag tells search engines what your page is about. 

When I say the meta title is short, you have roughly 45 to 60 characters, or around 600px to use when writing your title tag. 

Each character has its own pixel width. For example, a 'W' is wider than an 'I'. So a title tag containing 'W' 60 times would be too wide, but a title containing 'I' 60 times would fit. 

I was sad enough to test this individual character width limit in the past using a SERP simulator. I can confirm that a lower case 'W' fits 41 times, and a lower case 'I' fits 134 times. (It was a slow day.)

Because of this, there's not an exact number for your meta title to follow. As a rule of thumb, using around 45 to 60 characters is the sweet spot to avoid truncation.

Search engines will still index the whole meta title tag, but will truncate your title in search results if yours exceeds 600px. Therefore, it's a good idea to add your target keyword at the start of your meta title.

## **How to write your title tags for SEO**

The few words you have available for your title need to:

- Be keyword specific.
- Say what your page is about.
- Make searchers want to click through to your page.
- Reinforce your brand.

In order to optimise your title tag and make it effective you need to:

- **Decide the focus of your page:** Give it a single explicit subject. Make the subject of each page an inch wide and a mile deep. 
- **The page must support your website theme:** If you run a travel blog website, keep the focus on travel-themed topics. Adding pages about grandma's favourite cookie recipe would dilute your website's travel theme relevance.
- **Branding:** Some pages are critical to support your website's branding, while others are not. If branding is an issue on your site, include branded keywords in the title tag.
- **Keyword optimised:** Put keywords related to your website and page topic in the title. Leaving keywords out of your title tag hurts your chances to rank for the queries and searchers that are important to your site. 

Even though the length of your title tag can vary, you can follow some basic guidelines to get you started: 

1. Remember that your title tag needs to be unique and keyword rich.
2. The title of your page should belong only to that page. No duplicate titles.
3. Do not be sensational and promise things in your title that your page can't deliver.
4. Don't include keywords in your title that aren't relevant to your website and that you don't want to rank for.
5. Make sure you only have one title tag per page. Most CMS systems do this by default. If, however, you are adding title tags yourself via HTML, make sure not to create duplicate title tags.

### **Keyword optimising your meta title**

Put your keywords in at the beginning of your title to entice people to click through to your page. 

Search engines typically **bold search query words** that people type when they appear in your title, which can lead to better click-through rates. 

There are two types of keyword used for SEO. These are short-tail keywords and long-tail keywords. 

Short-tail keywords, as the name implies, are short in length, typically between 1-3 words, but can have very high search volume, usually in the thousands, and often in the hundreds of thousands. e.g. *"Apple MacBook Pro"*. 

You will likely find that pages optimised for short-tail keywords get lots of impressions, due to how often people search for those queries, but may struggle to get clicks to your site, because of the volume of competition these keywords have.

Long-tail keywords have 4+ words in them, and typically have far less search volume, sometimes as low as ten to one hundred searches a month. e.g. *"Keyboard and Screen Cleaner for Apple MacBook Pro"*. 

Long-tail keywords typically go the other way. Pages optimised for these queries usually have low impression counts, because not many people search for them, but your pages should receive a greater portion of the clicks, because you likely won't have as much competition for those keywords. 

The debate between short tail and long tail keywords is a hot topic in SEO, and there isn't a one size fits all approach. As a rule of thumb, people usually type short-tail queries when they first start looking for information, then use long-tail queries when they have the information and know what they are looking for. 

With this in mind, decide what step in the journey your page is best suited for, then keyword optimise your page for the stage of the journey your audience is in, and that your new page is most suitable for.

In practice this could be an information page, like a how-to-guide explaining how to use a product, which is optimised for a short-tail keyword. You could then create a second page optimised for a long-tail keyword, that your customer would likely search to find the specific product featured in your how-to-guide. (Make sure to add internal links between the two pages with keyword optimised anchor text for extra SEO bonus points.)

If you are trying to establish your brand then you can put your website or business name at the end of your title to use any remaining character space. This gives you a potential authority and trust boost for growing websites. It also gives your audience something they can quickly recognise.

## **Meta descriptions**

Although they're not a ranking factor, it is still a good idea to write meta descriptions for your page.

Search engines use your meta description tag to create the snippet you see beneath your page title in the search results. Google in particular is known to use your on-page text, or even write its own snippet to use for your search result if it feels your meta description isn't sufficient.

That said, your meta description is still an important tag to get right. A meta description is essentially a brief sentence explaining to visitors what they can expect to find on your page. It is best to write a unique, audience targeted and keyword optimised meta description for each page on your website.

The meta description has more character space than your title tag, around 130 to 160 characters. Or more accurately, 920px wide. The same rules on character pixel width in the meta title apply to the meta description. 

Also, similar to the title tag, words used in the user's search query that are in your meta description **may also be bolded** by search engines, meaning your page listing has another opportunity to catch the eye of searchers. This highlights the importance of keyword optimising your meta description. (Optimise, not stuff. Don't be spammy.)

### **What to write in your meta description**

A boring meta description with no hook or incentive makes it easy for people to scroll past and ignore you. Even worse, they click through to your competitor's page, adding fuel to your high impression, zero-click problem.

The main job of your meta description is to answer the question, "what is this page about?" 

Write naturally, and ask yourself what your target audience would be looking for when searching for a webpage like yours.

Another similarity between the meta title and meta description is the need to write unique descriptions that only that page uses. If you repeat meta descriptions across pages on your website, search engines may ignore them and write their own description instead. 

Duplicate titles and descriptions across multiple pages on your site mean search engines and readers can't tell them apart. If a search engine is unsure whether a page is relevant to a query, they will favour one that makes it easy for them to understand what the page is about.

In my experience, Bing Webmaster Tools is especially quick to flag duplicate meta titles and meta descriptions. Identical listings give searchers no reason to pick your page over competitors. Think of meta tags as your advertisement, and the content as your product. Don't be guilty of false advertising by copying a meta description from another page that doesn't accurately describe your new page.

Include a hook or reason to entice people to click through to your page. Don't say *"This page is about potatoes"*. Say something like, *"Do you love hot, golden, crispy roast potatoes? Grab our recipe to make fluffy and perfectly seasoned roasties... that are also super easy to make!".* Give searchers a tangible reason to click onto your page.

## **What does a good meta title and meta description look like?**

You now know the details when it comes to meta titles and meta descriptions. Here's how you can put that information into practice on your next product page or blog post.

### **Speak directly to your reader**

Speaking to everyone means you are speaking to no one. 

Write as if you are talking directly to the reader. Imagine you are having a one-on-one conversation with them. This is where having a target audience in mind is beneficial. Define who your dream customer is, and write for that person. 

For example, *“Learn how to fix your soggy roast potatoes”* speaks to someone more than *“Soggy roast potatoes can be fixed by following this formula”*. An active voice feels like you are talking directly to the reader. A passive voice sounds like you are speaking to the masses.

Don't give searchers a reason to scroll past your page by being passive. Use an active voice to sound more confident and speak directly to your audience.

### **Call to Action (CTA)**

I touched on calls to action (CTAs) earlier, with the roast potato example. Don't just describe the page. Tell searchers what they are going to gain. Your meta description in particular is a great place to add a CTA. What will they learn? What will they receive? How will their life benefit? Use words like "Discover", "Learn", "Fix", and so on, to entice people to click through to your page. 

### **Examples of meta tags for SEO**

The following are some example templates you can use to write your meta titles and meta descriptions. This isn't an exhaustive list, but using the examples below should help take some of the pressure off when you come to writing your own meta tags.

**Meta title:**

- **Standard Format:** [Primary Keyword] - [Secondary Keyword] | Brand Name 
- Example: *Roast Potatoes -* *Easy Meal Prep Recipes* | *Potatoes R Cool*
- **Service/Local Format:** [Service] or [Keyword] in [Location] | Brand Name 
- Example: *Sweet Potato Delivery* in *Cardiff* | *Cool Potato Farmer*
- **List/Guide Format:** [Number] + [Keyword] + [Benefit or Topic] | Brand Name 
- Example: *11 Types of Potatoes* to *Fuel Your Run* | *Spud Runner*
- **E-commerce Format:** [Product Name] - [Feature] | Brand Name 
- Example: Buy *Sweet Potatoes* - *High in Vitamin A* | *Peel the Force*
- **Blog/Article Format:** [Headline] or [Topic] | Brand Name 
- Example: *Are Potatoes a Superfood? The Evidence* | *White Coat Spud*

**Meta description:**

- **Problem-Solution-Benefit:** Address user pain point + Provide solution + Highlight key benefit + CTA 
- Example: Sick of eating bland potatoes? Try our freshly cooked jacket potatoes, loaded with your favourite toppings. Discover your next favourite meal!
- **Feature-Benefit:** List key features/specs + Explain user benefit + CTA
- Example: Sweet potatoes are high in vitamin A and fibre, supporting your eye health and digestion. Order your bag of sweet potatoes and boost your health today!
- **Question-Answer:** Anticipate search query + Provide direct answer/solution + CTA
- Example: Why are roast potatoes crispy outside and fluffy inside? Parboil, rough up the edges and use the right fat. Get our full method and try it yourself tonight!
- **Value-Proposition:** Unique selling point + Specific details (e.g., free shipping, price) + CTA
- Example: The only blue potatoes in the UK! Exclusive potatoes from Exotic Potatoes UK, with free next-day delivery. Discover the wonder of blue potatoes today.

I included brand names in those meta title examples to give you an example of how you could add yours. But you don't need to add yours if you don't want to, or if there's not enough space. Also, blue potatoes, not a thing. (But if they do become a thing, tell me!)

Search engines may still rewrite your tags even if you use one of these templates. Check your Search Console data after a few weeks to see if your click-through rate has improved. If impressions are steady and clicks go up, your new meta tags are working. Your new title tags could see you rank for keywords that you hadn't considered, so it's always good to track your performance over time.

## Meta tags for SEO FAQs

**How do I add meta tags for SEO?**

Most website platforms give you a box for them. 

In Shopify, you open the page you want to edit, find the search engine listing preview section, click Edit website SEO, then fill in the page title and description fields and save. 

Products and collections work the same way, with the search engine listing section sitting at the bottom of the editor. [Shopify’s own help centre](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/seo/adding-keywords) has the full steps on how you can add meta tags to your site.

[On WordPress](https://www.wpbeginner.com/beginners-guide/how-to-add-keywords-and-meta-descriptions-in-wordpress/), the usual route is an SEO plugin such as Yoast SEO or Rank Math. These plugins add a section below the editor where you can write your title and description.

**How do I edit meta tags for SEO?**

Using the fields you used to add them. On WordPress, open the page or post, scroll to the SEO plugin section below the editor, change your title or description, then update the page. 

On Shopify, your homepage title and description live under Online Store, then Preferences. For category and product pages, you simply edit the fields you used to add your meta tags.

## **Key Takeaways**

- Impressions but no clicks can point to weak meta tags. 
- The title tag is the most important meta element. 
- Keep titles unique, specific, and roughly 45–60 characters (\~600px). 
- Short-tail vs long-tail matters for the stage of the journey. 
- Meta descriptions aren’t a ranking factor. But they still matter. 
- A hook and a call to action make a description worth clicking.

Meta tags are an important part of your SEO. Well-written and keyword optimised meta tags can have a positive impact on your organic performance. Combine good meta tags with high-quality content and you are well on your way to driving traffic to your site.

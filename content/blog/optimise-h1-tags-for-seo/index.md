## Summary

It sucks working hard to create a new landing page full of great content, only to see the page underperform and not get the traffic you were hoping for. Something that can help you bring more visitors to your page is having a well optimised H1 tag. An H1 tag for SEO doesn't mean a lot to most people. But to search engines, they are an important piece of your landing page.

Having a clear and well written H1 helps search engines understand the topic of the page. A good H1 also tells your readers they are in the right place.

In this article, you will learn what to avoid and what to do when creating H1 tags, as well as how to research and write your own search engine optimised page header using only competitor research and some investigative nuance.

## Introduction to SEO H1 Tags

I didn't see myself writing a page about H1 tags. I thought it was something that was known and put into practice. But recently, I've found a few examples of poor SEO practice used on landing pages, and it's made me think that perhaps optimising H1 tags is not something to be taken for granted, and people need some help to understand and implement them effectively.

## What to avoid with your H1 tags

Let's first cover what inspired this page.

I worked for a company recently on their search engine optimisation, and I ran an audit on their site. The audit quickly found that nearly all of their blog pages had multiple H1 headers. I looked at the landing pages on their editing tool and found that every header was an H1 header, and all the paragraphs were H2s.

I asked the design team who had made the pages why they chose this "unique" heading structure, and they looked at me blankly and asked, *"Isn't that what you're supposed to do?"*.

It was at this moment I realised the scale of the task in front of me.

To understand why this isn't best practice when it comes to SEO, I want you to imagine a book. Picture the front cover with the title written proudly on the front. It clearly tells you the subject of the book. You feel confident this book is on the exact topic you want to read. You flick the pages through your fingers until you find the first chapter. But when you get there, you find another book cover, with a different title, kind of similar to the first, but not exactly the same. You now question whether this book is what you're looking for after all, and wonder, *"Is the book about this or about that?"*.

That's what happens when a search engine finds multiple H1 headers on a page. The main topic appears diluted. Search engines don't penalise you for having multiple H1 tags, but they can't confidently pin down what your page is about either if you do. They are then uncertain whether your page would satisfy the searcher's need if you were presented as the source to answer their query.

Each page should have one H1 header. That is the visible on-page heading you see at the top of the page (so for this page it's *"How to Write and Optimise H1 Tags for SEO"*). It tells the person reading the page what the topic is about.

On your CMS, you will likely see a text box called "Page Title", or "SEO Title", or something very similar. The H1 and the title tag are not the same thing.

The title tag lives in your landing page's HTML head section. The title tag is what you see in your browser tab, and is also the blue link headline on a search engine results page (referred to by SEOs as a "SERP"). The title tag in your landing code looks like this: `<title>Title here</title>`.

On your landing page's HTML code, the H1 tag looks like this: `<h1>Heading here</h1>`. They can use similar wording or phrases, but they are two different elements doing two different things.

What you type in the "Page Title" box can sometimes be copied by your CMS, so that it also becomes the H1 header you see on your landing page.

Unless you explicitly add an H1 header in the body of your page content, you can easily end up with the same H1 as your title tag, or a title tag and no H1 at all.

So when you are editing your page, make sure you check:

- The Page Title / SEO Title text box. This controls the browser tab text and search link text.
- The header at the top of your page's content. This should be the one and only H1 header on your page.

Once you have added your H1 header, make sure to use H2s and H3s for the rest of your page underneath.

A good title tag helps your page listing be understood and clicked in search results.

A good H1 header helps your readers know they are in the right place for what they are looking for.

You may be wondering at this point: how can I add an H1 header to my page if there is only one page title box?

In the main body area of your page editor (where the bulk of your written text goes, like what you're reading right now!), you will see options to format text into headers, usually a button labelled "headings". Simply highlight the text you want to use as the on-page header, and select H1 on the list of choices presented when you click "headings".

Usually on the far right of the same row of options where you find the "Headings" option, you will find a button called "Source Code".

When you click this button, the text will change and display the HTML version of your content. Here, you will see things like your paragraphs starting and ending with paragraph HTML tags (`<p></p>`), like so: `<p>Text goes between the tags here</p>`.

You'll find similar for additional heading tags, like H2, H3, etc. (`<h2>Header here</h2>`, swapping h2 for h3 to add H3 headers, and so on). What you are checking for here in the source code is that the text you just highlighted and made your 'Heading 1' now has H1 tags either side of the text (i.e. `<h1>Header Here</h1>`).

Going into the basics of HTML is beyond the scope of this page. But remember, one H1 header at the top of your page is all you need to optimise your content for clarity, accessibility, and page hierarchy (i.e. most to least important page sections). Search engines can cope with your page having multiple H1s, but sticking to one and using H2s and H3s etc is considered best practice.

## What to do to optimise H1 tags for SEO

So we've established that you only need one H1 header for your page. Great. But how exactly do you create a search engine optimised H1 header tag?

I'll use this article to explain my process for optimising header tags for SEO.

Like I said in the introduction, I saw a need for a page to be written explaining how to optimise H1 header tags. (And landing page content in general, but that's for another day.)

If there is a need for a page, there must already be people searching online for answers for that very need, to understand what to do and how to improve their pages.

So what are they searching? What do they type in the text box of a search engine or AI to get the information they need?

Well, the initial answer that gets the ball rolling is, what would *you* type into a search engine if *you* were looking for answers?

So to begin my research for this page I entered, *"How do I optimise a H1 tag for SEO?"*

It's good to see if there are already pages that exist that your new "competition" has written in order to answer that very question you're thinking of creating content for.

If there is competition then don't fret. It's a good sign. It means there is demand out there for what you're thinking of writing.

So why are we looking at what others have done?

You want to read the titles and pages that your competition has written in order to get a feel for what they cover on their pages. Chances are it's exactly what your audience is looking for, and there's a reason they show up in the top results.

By researching and understanding what the competition has written for their header tags, you discover if there are any nuances or deeper levels to the question that readers are also curious about and want to know.

Let's say for example that in addition to the search term I used to begin my research, *"How do I optimise a H1 tag for SEO?"*, I notice that the top ranking pages use that query for their header tag, but the first three positions go a bit deeper and have the title, *"How to write and keyword optimise a H1 tag for SEO."*

My original search query didn't include anything about keyword optimisation for header tags, but it appears that is what searchers are also interested in when they look for information for this query, so it must be an important factor. That is something you should take into consideration when writing your header tags, with thorough on-page content to back the H1 up and give readers what they want.

After reading the header examples from your competitor research, you will now be in a good place to brainstorm and create your SEO H1 header.

From here you could do further keyword research, and in some cases I would encourage that to give yourself further proof you're on the right track. But keyword research is beyond the scope of this page, and is a topic I will revisit in due course.

For now, let's say you have brainstormed some ideas for your new page header, and you have a first draft that you're happy with. Something like, *"H1 Tags: How to Write and Optimise your H1 for Maximum Traffic"*. How do you know if that is a suitable header for your own page in order to compete with the pages you found during your research?

A good place to start is entering your new H1 header into a search engine and analysing the search results page.

If you see the same or similar pages that appeared during your research for your new title then you know you're in the right neighbourhood with your H1 header.

## Key Takeaways

- Only use one H1 header on each landing page on your site.
- Multiple H1 headers can make your page look poorly structured. Use H2s, H3s etc for clarity, accessibility, and page hierarchy.
- The title tag and the H1 tag are not the same thing.
- To create your SEO header, think about what you would search to find the information if you were looking for answers.
- Research your competitors and read their headers and pages to understand what is currently working and ranking highly.
- Brainstorm and create your own H1 header taking inspiration from top performing pages.
- Enter your new H1 header in a search engine and check that the same or similar competitor pages you saw during your research show for your newly written header.

All you need is a little empathy and some investigative work and you will be well on your way to satisfying both people and robots with your H1 headers.
